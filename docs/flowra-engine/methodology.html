What this engine does

It scores a West African agricultural trade transaction out of 60 points and turns that score into an advance rate. Every point traces back to one rule, and every rule is stated below with the arithmetic that produces it.

Where the weights come from

The rules were written from domain expertise and sense-checked against 314 transactions recorded between January and April 2026. They were not fitted to that data. No regression was run, no weights were optimised, and no borrower in the base has yet completed a loan — so nothing here has been validated against repayment.

⚠ The weights are provisional.

Treat a score as a structured argument about a file, not a measurement of its risk. After 50 completed loans the weights should be recalibrated against what actually predicted repayment. The Caveats section sets out what the data can and cannot support.

One honest summary

Of everything measured, only price coherence shows a defensible relationship with transaction success, and even that is modest. Route risk is observable but barely discriminates. Borrower activity shows no relationship at all. The engine is weighted accordingly — the largest single rule is the price check.

Layer 1 — Price Coherence 25 points

The intuition

A declared price is the one number the lender can check independently. WACTAF records a median market price for each product on each route; if the seller's declared price sits near it, the cargo is worth roughly what they say, and the advance is anchored to something real. If it does not, the collateral value is unverifiable — and a price far from the market is the classic signature of an inflated invoice.

Concretely

Ginger declared at the WACTAF median reads as an honest file. The same ginger declared far above the median means either the seller does not know the market or the value is being inflated to borrow against. Neither is a file to fund at 70%.

The arithmetic

Rule 1A — Cohérence Prix (15 pts). A categorical field set by comparing the declared unit price against the WACTAF median.

IF Cohérence Prix = "✓ Cohérent" → +15 pts IF Cohérence Prix = "⚠ Légèrement hors" → +8 pts IF Cohérence Prix = "⚠ Hors norme" → 0 pts + MANDATORY DECLINE

Rule 1B — Spread Commercial (10 pts). The trading margin.

Spread % = (Resale value − Purchase cost) / Purchase cost × 100 IF 20 ≤ Spread ≤ 40 → +10 pts realistic IF 15 ≤ Spread < 20 or 40 < Spread ≤ 50 → +5 pts borderline OTHERWISE → 0 pts implausible + review flag

Below 15%, transport and levies consume the entire margin and the trade cannot service the advance. Above 50% is not observed on these corridors and usually means one side of the trade is mis-stated.

What the data shows

Cohérence PrixConformantRate
✓ Cohérent218 / 23294.0%
⚠ Légèrement hors39 / 4292.9%
⚠ Hors norme36 / 4090.0%

The gradient runs the right way — coherent pricing performs best — and the odds ratio between coherent and out-of-range pricing is 1.73. This is the strongest relationship in the dataset, which is why Rule 1A carries the single largest weight.

⚠ But keep it in proportion.

The gap is 4 percentage points across 314 records, and even out-of-range pricing succeeded 90% of the time. On a sample this size that difference is not statistically decisive. It is suggestive, consistent with the operational logic, and entirely unvalidated against repayment.

Worked example

Declared value 900,000 FCFA Purchase cost 650,000 FCFA Cohérence Prix ✓ Cohérent Spread = (900,000 − 650,000) / 650,000 × 100 = 38.5% Rule 1A ✓ Cohérent → +15 Rule 1B 38.5% → within 20–40% → +10 ──── Layer 1 subtotal 25 / 25

Layer 2 — Route Risk 20 points

The intuition

Corridors are not equal. Road quality, border formalities, and checkpoint density vary, and each leaves a trace in how often cargo arrives conformant. The product matters too: the question is how fast goods lose value if the trip is delayed, and how readily they resell if the buyer walks away.

The arithmetic

Rule 2A — Corridor (9 pts). A lookup against observed routes.

Established corridor (in the observed base) → +9 pts Rare corridor (fewer than ~15 deals) → +5 pts + review flag Untested corridor (absent from the base) → +5 pts + MANDATORY DECLINE

Rule 2B — Product type (6 pts).

Produits Agricoles → +6 pts storable, deepest resale market Produits Bétails → +5 pts mortality and feed costs en route Produits Halieutiques → +4 pts highly perishable, cold-chain dependent Unclassified → 0 pts + MANDATORY DECLINE

Rule 2C — Corridor incidents (5 pts).

IF incidents = 0 → +5 pts IF incidents = 1 → +2 pts + insurance requirement IF incidents ≥ 2 → 0 pts

The entire spread across established corridors is about 5 percentage points. Route is a real but weak discriminator — it earns 9 of 60 points, not more.

⚠ A 100% corridor is not a safe corridor.

Niger → Côte d'Ivoire (14 deals) and Nigeria → Côte d'Ivoire (5 deals) have perfect records. On samples that small, a perfect record is what you expect from a ~94% route by chance — roughly a 43% chance over 14 deals. Both are scored as rare (5 points), not excellent, and both raise a manual-approval flag. Rewarding them for a success rate the data cannot support would be the single easiest way to lose money with this engine.

By product type: Agricoles 154/165 (93.3%), Bétails 138/147 (93.9%), Halieutiques 1/1 (100%). The first two are statistically indistinguishable; the third is a single transaction and carries no information. The ordering in Rule 2B rests on the perishability argument, not on this data.

⚠ Why incidents are not weighted more heavily.

Nb Incidents correlates with failure at r = −0.687, far stronger than anything else measured. Every one of the 11 transactions with a recorded incident was non-conformant; all 303 with none were 96.7% conformant.

That is too clean to be foresight. An "incident" appears to be recorded as part of the same failure it would be predicting — so using it as a forward-looking signal leaks the outcome into the score. It would look superb in backtest and predict nothing at application time, when no incident has yet occurred. It is therefore capped at 5 points as a history check on the borrower's past route record, not treated as the dominant predictor the correlation would suggest.

Worked example

Corridor Togo → Bénin (70 deals, 94.3%) Product Produits Agricoles Incidents 0 Rule 2A established corridor → +9 Rule 2B Agricoles → +6 Rule 2C no incidents → +5 ──── Layer 2 subtotal 20 / 20

Layer 3 — Borrower Activity 15 points

⚠ Read this before the rules: this layer does not predict success.

In the observed base, trading activity has no relationship with whether a transaction completes conformant. Prior trips correlate at r = −0.067 — slightly negative — and monthly frequency at r = +0.004, which is zero. The layer is kept for portfolio management: preferring established repeat traders over one-offs is a defensible commercial policy. It is not a risk measurement, and it is deliberately capped at a quarter of the score.

The arithmetic

Rule 3A — Repeat trader (6 pts) trips ≥ 3 → +6 trips = 2 → +3 trips = 1 → 0 trips = 0 → 0 pts + MANDATORY DECLINE Rule 3B — Frequency (5 pts) ≥ 2 / month → +5 1–1.9 / month → +2 < 1 → 0 Rule 3C — Specialization (4 pts) concentration ≥ 80% → +4 50–79% → +2 < 50% → 0 where concentration = trips on primary corridor / total trips

There is no monotone pattern here. Two trips outperforms three; the most active trader in the base (47 trips) sits below the one-trip group. With 223 of 314 records at a single trip, there is barely any variation to learn from in the first place.

Why the relationship is absent

Most likely selection: every borrower in the base already passed a screen, so the variation that would separate good from bad was removed before the data was recorded. This is a reason to distrust the measurement, not evidence that experience is worthless. It should be re-tested once there is outcome variation to test against.

Hard stops override the score

Five conditions decline a file no matter how well it scores. The points are still shown, so a reviewer can see how the file looked, but the advance is forced to zero.

Worked example — a full file

Ginger · Togo → Bénin · declared 900,000 FCFA Layer 1 coherent price, 38.5% spread 25 / 25 Layer 2 established corridor, agricoles, no incidents 20 / 20 Layer 3 5 trips, 2.1/month, 85% focus 15 / 15 ─────── Total 60 / 60 Tier ★★★ Approved Advance rate 70% Max advance 900,000 × 0.70 = 630,000 FCFA Tenor 14 days
⚠ Tier boundaries are brittle.

A 39 and a 40 differ by one rule but by 15 percentage points of advance. The engine flags any file within two points of a threshold, so the fragility is visible rather than hidden behind a tier label. Nothing in the data justifies the thresholds sitting at exactly 30, 40 and 50 — they are round numbers pending calibration.

⚠ Caveat 1 — This measures delivery, not repayment.

The 93.3% is the share of cargo that arrived conformant. It is not a repayment rate. No borrower in the base has completed a loan, so nothing in this engine has ever been tested against the outcome it exists to predict. A file can deliver perfectly and still not be repaid.

⚠ Caveat 2 — The sample is small and one-sided.

314 records over four months, with only 21 failures in total. Credit models are normally built on thousands. With so few failures, no rule here can be estimated with meaningful precision — and four months captures no seasonality, so lean-season behaviour is entirely unobserved.

⚠ Caveat 3 — A 93.3% success rate is suspiciously high.

Every borrower in the base already passed a screen before being recorded. The failures that would teach the model most are probably missing, which both inflates the success rate and flattens the differences between rules. This is the most likely explanation for Layer 3 showing no signal.

⚠ Caveat 4 — Two inputs are derived, not measured.

The workbook has no corridor-concentration column, so concentration is computed as each seller's share of trips on their most-used corridor. Its Trips/Mois column reads 30 even for sellers with a single trip — a days-in-period constant, not a frequency — so frequency is derived as trips over the four-month window instead. Both derivations are reasonable; neither is the operator's own measurement.

Glossary

Cohérence Prix
A categorical judgement of the declared unit price against the WACTAF market median for that product and route: Cohérent, Légèrement hors, or Hors norme. The single heaviest input in the engine.
Spread Commercial
The trading margin, (resale − purchase) / purchase × 100. Under 15% leaves nothing to repay from once transport and levies are met; over 50% is not observed on these corridors.
Corridor
An origin→destination country pair, e.g. Togo → Bénin. Scored on how reliably cargo has completed on that route in the observed base.
Corridor concentration
Share of a seller's trips running on their most-used corridor. A trader with 4 of 5 trips on one route is at 80% — a specialist who knows its checkpoints and buyers.
Conformant delivery
Cargo arrived as declared (Livraison Conforme = Oui). This is the outcome measured in the base — not loan repayment.
Advance rate
Share of declared cargo value released to the borrower up front: 70 / 55 / 40% by tier. The remainder is the lender's buffer against the cargo being worth less than declared.
Tenor
Maximum days to repayment, 7 to 14 by tier.
Mandatory decline
A hard stop that forces the advance to zero regardless of points.
PD — Probability of Default
The statistical likelihood a borrower fails to repay. Not yet computable here: it needs completed loans with known outcomes, and there are none. It is the metric phase 3 exists to produce.
Point-biserial correlation (r)
Association between a numeric input and a yes/no outcome, from −1 to +1. Zero means no linear relationship. Reported above for each activity variable — all near zero.

Reproducing these figures

Every statistic on this page was computed directly from the 314-record workbook: success rates by category, the 1.73 odds ratio, and each correlation. The rule tables match js/scoring-engine.js as deployed. The underlying workbook is not published — it carries borrower names, phone numbers and vehicle plates.
